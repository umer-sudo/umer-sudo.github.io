Ad account read sheetPrintable · ten minutes

Start at the money.

The top of an ad account is where the platform puts the numbers that make you feel good. This sheet starts at the other end and walks backwards: what came back, what it cost, which creative earned it, what the page did with the click.

The article, with the worked examples →
The sheetThirteen steps

In this order, every time.

  1. Work out break-even before you open anything

    Divide one by your gross margin: that is how many dollars must come back per dollar spent. Without it, every number is compared to zero.

  2. Find what came back per dollar, for the whole account

    Ignore the platform’s name for it and find the ratio. It is the only opening question.

  3. Compare the return to break-even, not to zero

    A return that looks healthy loses money at a thin margin and looks modest at a fat one. Your margin decides, not the chart.

  4. Find the cost to win one customer

    The ratio hides volume. A beautiful return on a handful of orders is a rounding error with a graph attached.

  5. Set that cost against a year of the customer, not one order

    A customer who comes back four times justifies far more spend than the first order suggests. One who never returns justifies less.

  6. Sort the creative by spend, highest first

    Only the pieces carrying the budget have enough data to mean anything. Sort by performance and the platform shows you a lucky ad that cost very little.

  7. Read the top few and stop

    Anything under a few hundred dollars of spend is a rumour, not a result. Do not let it into the verdict.

  8. Check how much of the spend one piece carries

    One ad carrying most of the budget is not a creative strategy. It is a countdown.

  9. Write down what the winners share

    Usually the first two seconds, not the offer. That line is the brief for the next round.

  10. Compare arrivals with checkouts

    This is where most accounts are actually broken and the last place people look. More budget buys more visits to a leaking bucket.

  11. Ask where the sale actually closes

    On some businesses it closes in an inbox or on the phone, not on the page. Page conversions there show a broken account and a healthy business.

  12. Ignore impressions, reach, engagement rate and raw clicks

    They rise with spend and cannot fall while the budget holds, which is exactly why they lead the screen.

  13. Write the verdict in one line

    Fails at the cost step: the offer or the margin, and no creative will save it. Fails at the page step: the page. Knowing which is most of the work.
