The comparisonThe short answer

Instagram DMs or a checkout

Sell through DMs when the buyer has a question before they pay and would rather ask it than read; in Pakistan that is most buyers, and the order is paid in cash at the door. Sell through a checkout when the product needs no conversation and the buyer is abroad or paying by card. Most brands here need the inbox first and the store when the questions start repeating.

Why does the sale close in the inbox in Pakistan?

Because the buyer was already shopping that way before your ad arrived. People here buy from people on WhatsApp, pay at the door, and ask before they commit: the size, the spelling, the delivery day, whether the colour matches the picture. An Instagram inbox is WhatsApp with a shopfront on it, and a checkout asks that buyer to change how they shop to buy from you. Most will not. The brands that sell well have turned the inbox into the shop window, with the price list in the bio and the saved replies ready, so the conversation starts close to the sale.

What changes about the measurement?

Everything the ad platform relies on. A pixel sees a purchase on a page and nothing of a message, a reply or a rider paid in cash, so a campaign optimised for website purchases is optimising for a thing that barely happens. The campaign has to be optimised toward messages, and the orders logged from the inbox, matched to the creative that started them and carried through to the courier, with refusals at the door subtracted before anyone calls it a return. The Gallery was counted that way, and every campaign to July 2026 returned $5.20–$6 for every $1 of ads.

When should a DM-first brand add a store?

When the inbox has become the bottleneck rather than the advantage. The signs: the same five questions arrive daily; the catalogue has grown past what a highlight can hold; orders die because nobody replied in time; buyers abroad want to pay by card; or you want prepaid orders to cut refusals at the door. Then the store takes the repeat questions and the ready buyers, and the inbox keeps the ones who need to ask. Seyr is a storefront built that way, around what a buyer needs to see and in what order, client-run from the first day.

How we run both without losing either

  1. Count the order where it closes

    Placed in a message, confirmed with an address, delivered and paid. Only the last is revenue, and the ads are judged on it.

  2. Answer the repeat questions on a page

    Sizes, delivery days, the price list, the return terms. Every question answered on the page is one fewer in the inbox and one fewer order lost waiting.

  3. Point the ad at the right door

    A product bought on sight goes to the page; a product that needs a conversation goes to the inbox. One campaign each, so the number stays legible.

  4. Confirm before dispatch

    A reply with the address, and a phone number that was called rather than assumed. Cash on delivery leaks between the message and the door, and the confirmation is the plug.

  5. Give a reason to prepay

    A small discount or free delivery for card payment moves the buyers who can, and every prepaid order is one the rider cannot bring back.

Cash on delivery, plainly

Cash on delivery is why the inbox works and why it leaks. The buyer commits nothing until the rider is at the door, which makes the first message easy and the last step uncertain: the mind changes, the phone is off, the address was a landmark, the courier tried once. Each refusal costs the delivery out, the return in, the packaging and the day the product could have sold to someone else, and none of it appears in the ad platform. So the delivered rate, not the message count, is the figure that says whether the campaign paid. A brand that confirms every order and chases the delivery sees ads that pay; a brand that counts messages watches a good campaign lose money at the door.

FAQ
Do I need a website to run Meta ads in Pakistan?

No. A campaign can be pointed at the inbox and measured there. A page helps once the questions repeat, and it is where the pixel finally has something to see.

How do you track sales that happen in DMs?

Orders are logged from the inbox with the creative that started them, carried to the courier sheet and reported weekly against the number agreed, with refusals taken off.

Can a store and the inbox run at the same time?

Yes, and most brands here end up there: the page for the ready buyer, the inbox for the one with a question, and one count that covers both.

Does Drifted reply to the DMs?

We hand your team the reply script and the confirmation steps, and we can cover the first hour after a post if you want. The replies are best done by people who know the stock.
