Offshore agency or a US agency

A US agency gives you the same hours by default, a native ear for your market and a contract you can enforce at home. An offshore agency gives you more senior attention per dollar, provided the overlap is written down and the assets stay yours. We are in Karachi, keep New York hours, and will tell you when the local shop is the better buy.

When should a US brand hire locally?

When someone has to be in the room: shoots on your premises, retail activations, in-person sales support. When decisions are hourly rather than daily, as in live commerce, ticketing or breaking-news reaction, because an overlap window does not cover a whole day. When the category is regulated and the compliance knowledge is local. And when the copy is the whole product and depends on regional humour or the internal language of a profession we have not worked inside. In those cases a local writer or a local shop is the better instrument, and we say so rather than take the scope and hope.

When does an offshore agency make sense?

When the work is media, production and measurement, which travel well, and the cultural ear is tested on a paid brief before you commit. When the overlap window is written into the contract as hours in your time zone rather than promised on a call. When the ad spend stays on your card, the accounts stay in your name and the notice period is short enough that a bad month is the worst case. Under those conditions the geography is a logistics question, and what you are buying is more senior attention for the same fee than a Manhattan retainer would get you.

How to test an offshore agency before you sign

  1. Message at the edge of the window

    Send a non-urgent question at the end of the stated overlap and time the reply. An agency that misses its own window while pitching will not improve after signing.

  2. Pay for a test brief

    A real product, a real audience, judged by you as a customer rather than as a buyer of marketing. Then ask who reviewed the copy and whether they are from your market.

  3. Ask who holds the spend

    If the answer is anything but your own card, paid to the platform, stop.

  4. Ask for the handover document

    A redacted account document from another client shows whether anything exists outside one person’s head.

  5. Read the notice clause

    Thirty days is reasonable. A year with an exit fee is a bet against you leaving.

What a fair offshore contract contains

  • An overlap window stated as hours in your time zone, with the days it applies.
  • A named team, and notice before anyone on it changes.
  • Advertising spend paid by you to the platform, never routed through the agency.
  • Every account, page and file in your name from the first day.
  • Monthly billing and thirty days’ notice, so the worst case is one wasted month.
  • A flat fee that does not move with your spend.

The questions that follow.

FAQ
Is an offshore agency just a cheaper US agency?

No. It is the same seniority at a lower fee, with hours and cultural fit that have to be tested rather than assumed. If price is your only reason, hire locally; cost alone produces the cheapest bid, not the right one.

Which hours do you overlap with New York?

The New York working day. The studio in Karachi starts in the local afternoon and runs through the American morning and afternoon, and that is written into the contract.

Who holds the advertising spend?

You do, on your own card, paid to the platform. We never route spend through our own accounts.

What happens if we want out?

Thirty days’ notice. The accounts, the pages and the creative were always in your name, so leaving is a message rather than a migration.

Will the copy sound American?

For media, production and measurement, yes, and you can test it on a paid brief. For copy that depends on deep regional nuance we will propose a writer from your market and run the rest.
