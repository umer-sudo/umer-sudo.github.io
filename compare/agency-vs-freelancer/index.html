Agency or freelancer for your marketing

A freelancer is the right buy when you need one skill, already make your own creative and can brief well. An agency is the right buy when media, creative and measurement have to sit in one place and someone has to pick up the account when a person is ill. We are an agency, and below we say plainly when the freelancer wins.

When is a freelancer the better choice?

When the job is one skill and you know which one. A brand with its own photographer and a founder who writes well needs someone to buy media, and a good freelance media buyer is cheaper and closer than any agency. When the budget is small enough that an agency fee would be most of it. When you want one named person and will accept that the account stops when they do. And when you are early enough that what you need is a hand on the account, not a bench behind it. In those cases we will say so on the call.

When is an agency the better choice?

When the work needs more than one skill in the same week. Ads need creative, creative needs a brief, and both need someone reading the numbers, and a freelancer with eight clients cannot be all three. When you have already had the year where the account plateaued and nobody was responsible for the ideas. When a holiday or a resignation would stop your marketing. And when you want the figures reported against a number that was written down, by people who will still be there when the number is bad. That is the arrangement we sell, and it costs more than a freelancer for the reasons above.

How to decide in a week

  1. Write the number

    Orders a month, enquiries a week, revenue by the quarter. If you cannot write it, neither option can hit it.

  2. List the skills the number needs

    Media, creative, copy, measurement, email. One skill points to a freelancer; three or more points to an agency or to a hire.

  3. Count your own hours

    Briefing, approving and chasing have a cost, and it is the founder’s hourly rate. A freelancer needs more of them than an agency does.

  4. Ask both for a month-one plan

    Not a deck. What runs, where, and what it should show by week four. The plan that names a number is the one that has looked.

  5. Pick the one you can stop

    Short notice, accounts in your name, no setup fee that locks you in. Whoever you choose, the exit should cost a month.

Questions that expose a bad agency or a bad freelancer

  • Who, by name, will read my account every week?
  • What happens to my campaign if that person is ill for a fortnight?
  • Is the fee flat, or does it grow with my ad spend?
  • What was the last account you lost, and why?
  • Can I see the figures from a current client, with the client named and the period stated?
  • Which parts of this would you tell me to do myself?

FAQ
Is an agency always more expensive than a freelancer?

On the invoice, almost always. On the full cost, including your own hours and the creative you would otherwise have to source, the gap is smaller than it looks and sometimes reverses.

Can I start with a freelancer and move to an agency later?

Yes, and it is a sensible order. Keep the accounts in your own name from day one so the move is a handover, not a rebuild.

Does Drifted work alongside a freelancer?

Yes. A freelance photographer or a specialist copywriter from your market often does one part better than we would, and we would rather say so than take the whole scope.

What does Drifted cost compared with a freelancer?

$2,000 a month for one discipline, which is more than most freelancers charge. The fee buys creative made for the account, weekly reading and cover, and it never takes a share of your spend.
