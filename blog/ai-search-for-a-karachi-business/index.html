AI search for a Karachi business

Where a Karachi customer actually looks, why the Business Profile comes first, the one-name-one-address rule, the pages an assistant will quote, and what to do if you sell in DMs and take cash at the door.

By Umer Tariq, founderPublished 22 October 20268 min read

A Karachi customer looking for what you sell no longer types a phrase and reads ten links. They open Maps and look at the pin. They search Instagram for the product and judge the profile in seconds. They ask a friend on WhatsApp. And, more every month, they ask an assistant and read the one answer it gives. If your business is not in that answer, it does not matter how good the feed is. This is what to do about it, for a business that may never have had a website at all.

The short version

Fix the Google Business Profile first, because it is the page a Karachi customer sees before any other. Use one name, one address and one phone number, spelled identically everywhere. Put the answers to the questions people ask you in DMs on pages of your own, in plain sentences, with the price and the delivery terms stated. An assistant cannot read your inbox or your pinned post; it can read a page.

Where does a Karachi customer actually look?

In four places, and they are not the four an agency usually optimises.

  • Maps and the Business Profile. For anything with a door, a cafe, a clinic, a tailor, a showroom, the search happens on the map and the decision is made on the listing: open now, how far, what it looks like, what people said.
  • Instagram search and the profile. For anything sold by post or by rider, the customer searches the product, lands on a profile, and judges it on the bio, the highlights and the pinned post before sending a message.
  • WhatsApp. The forward from a friend is the most trusted search result in the city, and it usually carries a screenshot of one of the above.
  • The assistants. ChatGPT, Google's AI answers and the rest, asked in English or in Roman Urdu: where do I get this, who does that in Clifton, is this shop genuine. The answer names a handful of businesses, and it names them from what it can read.

The first three a Karachi business already knows about. The fourth is new, and it is built on the same material as the first: whatever is written down about you, consistently, somewhere a machine can read it.

Why does the Business Profile come first?

Because it is the one page about your business that Google writes into the map, into search and into its own AI answers, and most Karachi businesses have never claimed it, or claimed it once and let it rot. A profile with the wrong hours, an old phone number and three photographs of the signboard is worse than none, because the customer trusts it and is let down at the door.

Claim it. Set the category to the thing you actually sell, not the broadest thing you could. Put the hours in and keep them true through Ramazan and Eid, when every listing in the city goes wrong at once. Add photographs of the product, the inside and the people, not the sign. Answer the questions that appear on it, because the answers are read by the assistant as well as the customer. Post to it from the feed a few times a month, so it is visibly alive. Ask every happy customer for a review in a message, with the link, the day the order arrives.

The Profile is the first page an assistant trusts about a local business, because it is the one Google has verified. Everything else you write is read against it.

What is the one address, one name rule?

Your business has one name, spelled one way, one address written one way, and one phone number formatted one way, and all three are identical on the Business Profile, the Instagram bio, the Facebook page, the website footer, the delivery app, the marketplace listing and the invoice. Identical, character for character. Not the Roman Urdu spelling on one and the English on another, not a mobile number here and a landline there, not Phase Eight in one place and an abbreviation in the next.

The reason is that an assistant decides whether two mentions are the same business by matching those three things. A business that calls itself three different names across five platforms looks like three businesses to a machine, each with a third of the evidence, and none of them gets quoted.

Pick the name today. Write the address once, as the post office would, and paste it everywhere. It is the cheapest search work there is, and in Karachi it is almost never done.

Which pages get cited?

The page that answers one question, in the first paragraph, in sentences a machine can lift without editing. An assistant composing an answer about where to buy a thing in Karachi is looking for the page that says, plainly, what the thing is, what it costs, where it is delivered and how to order. It is not looking for a home page that welcomes you to a world.

So the pages a local business needs are the questions its inbox gets, one per page.

Each page carries the same name and address as the Profile, says what it says in the first lines, and uses headings that are the question a person would type. A site of six honest pages gets cited. A site of sixty vague ones does not.

What if you sell in DMs, cash on delivery?

Most of Karachi does, and this is where the search work is most neglected, because the business feels it has no website to optimise. It is also where it pays most, because the assistant cannot read the one place the business has put its information. A pinned post with the price list is invisible to a machine. A highlight with the delivery areas is invisible. The saved reply that answers every question is the best-written thing the business owns, and it lives inside an inbox nobody outside can see.

The fix is to take what the saved replies already say and put it on pages. The price list becomes the prices page. The delivery reply becomes the delivery page, with cash on delivery stated plainly as a method and the terms stated beside it, because an assistant asked whether a shop does cash on delivery will quote the page that says so. The how-do-I-order reply becomes a page with the message link on it, so the assistant sends the customer into your inbox, which is where the sale was always going to close.

Nothing about the DM-first way of selling changes. The ad still starts a conversation and the sale still closes in a message. What changes is that the conversation can now begin from an answer an assistant gave, and that answer is drawn from pages you wrote rather than from whichever competitor wrote them first.

What does Drifted do for a local business?

The work above, in order, as the search option of our Starter tier or as part of the full loop. We claim and rebuild the Business Profile and keep it posted. We list the questions the inbox gets and write a page for each, in the plain sentences an assistant can quote, with the price and the delivery terms on them. We put the one name and one address everywhere it appears and fix every place it differs. We add a summary file to the site written for machines to read, and we check, every month, whether the assistants are naming the business when asked the questions its customers ask.

We do the same for ourselves. This site answers each question in its first paragraph, carries one description of the studio everywhere, and publishes its fees, which is part of why it gets quoted. Two pages on it say the rest: the Karachi agency page at /pakistan/marketing-agency-karachi, which says what the fee buys for a business here, and the AI search page at /services/ai-search, which lays out the method in full.

When is this not worth paying for?

When the business has no website and no intention of one; the Profile and the one-name rule are a morning's work the owner can do alone, and we would say so on the call. When the name changes every season, because no amount of page writing survives a business that renames itself. When nobody searches for the product at all, and the job is ads and a feed, not search. And when the owner cannot answer the inbox, because an assistant that sends a customer into an empty inbox has sent them to a competitor.

Everyone else is leaving the answer to someone else. A Karachi customer asked an assistant where to buy what you sell this morning. The assistant answered. The only question is whether the business it named was yours, and the answer to that is written on pages, or it is not.
