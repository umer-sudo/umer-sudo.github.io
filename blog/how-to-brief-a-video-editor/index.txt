38:["$","h2","2",{"id":"one-idea","className":"disp","children":"What is the one-idea-per-beat rule?"}]
39:["$","p","3",{"children":"A beat is a few seconds of video that carries one thing: one object, one claim, one movement, one line. A short video is three or four beats. The rule is that each beat carries one idea and no beat carries two. If a beat has to say the product is handmade and that it ships tomorrow, it is two beats, or one beat about whichever matters more."}]
3a:["$","p","4",{"children":"The rule exists because a viewer on a phone decides to stay or go several times inside a single video, and each decision is made on whichever idea is in front of them. Two ideas in one beat means neither lands, and the viewer leaves on the confusion. The editor cannot fix this in the cut. The brief has to arrive already split into beats, each one a line, in order."}]
3b:["$","p","5",{"children":"Write them as a list. Beat one: the overcoat, close, in the case. Beat two: the name on the collar. Beat three: the winter he wore it through. Beat four: the museum's name. An editor who receives that list knows what every shot is for before opening the footage."}]
3c:["$","h2","6",{"id":"hook","className":"disp","children":"What does the hook have to do?"}]
3d:["$","p","7",{"children":"Earn the second second. The first second of a short video is the whole audition; a viewer who is not held by it is already gone. So the hook is the single most interesting frame or line in the piece, and it goes first, before the logo, before the context, before anything that assumes the viewer has decided to watch."}]
3e:["$","p","8",{"children":"The brief names the hook. Not something attention-grabbing, which asks the editor to guess what you find interesting, but the actual shot or line. Open on the hand turning the collar over to show the name. Open on the caption that says he wore this for the whole of that winter. If you cannot name the hook, you do not yet know what the video is about, and the editor is about to find that out the expensive way."}]
3f:["$","p","9",{"children":"A logo is never the hook, nor a slow fade from black, nor a title card with the name of the series. Each tells the viewer the interesting part is later, and the viewer takes you at your word and leaves."}]
40:["$","h2","10",{"id":"captions","className":"disp","children":"Why write the captions for a muted viewer?"}]
41:["$","p","11",{"children":"Because most people watching on a phone have the sound off, in a queue, in a lift, in a meeting they are pretending to attend. If the idea is only in the voice, it did not reach them. The caption carries the idea; the sound is for the people who turned it on."}]
42:["$","p","12",{"children":"This changes what the brief contains. The caption text is written in the brief, word for word, per beat, before the edit starts. Short lines, one per beat, in the order the beats land. The editor's job is to place them and time them, not to write them, because the person who knows what the video is about is the person briefing it. A video briefed without caption text comes back with a transcript of the voice, which is the wrong thing: a transcript is what someone said; a caption is what the viewer needs to know."}]
43:["$","p","13",{"children":"Write the captions, read them with the sound off in your head, and ask whether the video still makes sense. If it does, the brief is nearly finished."}]
