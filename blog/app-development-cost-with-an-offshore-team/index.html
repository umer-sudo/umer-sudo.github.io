What app development costs with an offshore team

What actually drives the price of an app built offshore, the costs that do not show up in the quote, and the contract terms that protect a startup.

By Umer Tariq, founderPublished 28 October 20263 min read

Founders look offshore for one reason: the same budget buys more building. That is true, and it is also where the trouble starts, because a lower day rate on a badly scoped project is still an expensive project. The cost of an app is set far more by what you ask for, and how clearly, than by where the team sits.

The short version

An app's cost is driven by scope first: how many kinds of user, whether money moves through it, how many platforms, and what it connects to. An offshore team lowers the rate, not the scope. The hidden costs are time-zone delay, rework from a loose brief, and code you do not own. Fix all three in the contract: overlapping hours, a written scope tied to how the app earns, and the code in your own account from day one.

Fixed price or time and materials?

A fixed price suits a narrow, well-defined first version: the builder carries the risk of overrun, and you carry the risk that the scope was wrong. Time and materials suits a product that will change as you learn, but it needs a weekly view of hours and progress or it drifts. A sensible middle path is a fixed price for a written first version and a monthly retainer after launch.

What costs do not show up in the quote?

  • Store accounts: Apple's developer programme charges a yearly fee and Google Play a one-time fee, both in your name.
  • Hosting, databases, email sending, maps and other paid services, billed monthly as the app grows.
  • Maintenance: operating system updates break things every year, and someone has to fix them.
  • Your own time: answering questions, testing builds and making decisions. A slow answer stalls a fast team.
  • Rework when the brief said one thing and meant another.

Does the time zone matter?

More than the rate. A team that sleeps through your working day turns every question into a day's delay, and a project with many small questions loses weeks that way. Ask for a few hours of overlap at minimum, or a team that works your hours outright. Our studio is in Karachi and works New York business hours, precisely so a founder in the United States gets answers the same afternoon.

What should the contract say?

  1. The code, the design files and every account are yours from the first day, in repositories and stores you own.
  2. A written scope for the first version, tied to how the app earns money.
  3. What happens to bugs found after launch, and for how long.
  4. Who the named people on the project are, and that they cannot be swapped silently.
  5. Thirty days' notice either way, with a clean handover.

How do we price a build?

Per build, under our Tailored tier, to the number the app is meant to produce, because a marketplace with payments and trust features is not the same project as a booking tool for one clinic. For ehsaan, a person-to-person marketplace app for Pakistan, we wrote a 19-section knowledge base before building and then reached the first build. The revenue model is written before the first screen, and the client keeps the code.

When is offshore the wrong call?

When the founder cannot write down what the first version must do, or has no time to answer questions each week. Distance magnifies vagueness. In that case, spend first on a short paid scoping exercise, wherever the team is, and only then decide who builds it.
