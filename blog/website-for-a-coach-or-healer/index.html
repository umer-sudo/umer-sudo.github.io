What a website for a coach or healer needs

The pages, the words and the contact setup that turn a recommendation into a first session, for coaches, healers, mediums and anyone whose business is themselves.

By Umer Tariq, founderPublished 27 October 20263 min read

A coach or a healer is usually found by name. Someone hears about you from a friend, a talk or a post, and searches for you to check you are real and to see what you offer. The website's job is to answer that visitor quickly, in your own voice, and make the first contact easy. Everything else is optional.

The short version

Four pages: who you help, what you offer, who you are, how to reach you. Write them in the words you use with clients. Give each offering its own page with what happens in a session. Use a simple contact form unless your diary is fixed. Answer the questions a first-time client is too shy to ask.

Which pages does a coach or healer need?

PageWhat it must answer
HomeWho you help, with what, and how to start
Each offeringWhat happens, how long it takes, online or in person, and how to ask
AboutHow you came to this work, told plainly and truthfully
ContactOne easy way to reach you, and when you reply
QuestionsWhat first-time clients wonder but rarely ask

Should the site have online booking?

Only if your diary is fixed enough to keep it honest. A practitioner who travels, or who prefers to speak to someone before booking them, is better served by a contact form and a prompt reply. Channeling with Emily, a website we built for a psychic medium and healer in California, uses a contact form rather than a booking calendar for exactly that reason. It is in final review.

How should the words be written?

In your own voice, from your own material. Your bio, a recording of a talk, the way you explain a session to a nervous first-timer: these are better raw material than anything a template offers. Keep the claims to what you can stand behind. Say what a session involves and how clients describe feeling afterwards, rather than promising outcomes.

How do people find a coach or healer by search?

Two ways. By name, which needs only a site that loads fast and says the same thing as your social profiles. And by question: what is a reading, what happens in a healing session, can it be done online. A short page answering each question in plain words is what both search engines and AI assistants quote.

What changes between the US, Europe and Pakistan?

  • In the United States, say plainly whether sessions are online, in person or both, and in which state.
  • In the Netherlands and the rest of the European Union, a contact form collects personal data, so publish a short privacy note saying what you keep and why.
  • In Pakistan, many clients will reach you on WhatsApp before they use a form, so put the number where they can find it.

When is hiring a studio the wrong call?

When you are still finding out what you offer. A simple page on a site builder, written in your own words, is enough to test whether people book. Hire help when the offerings are settled and the site is what stands between a recommendation and a first session.
