How a Karachi studio works with US businesses across timezones

The time difference between Karachi and the United States, what it costs a client, what it quietly gives them, and the working rules that make it hold.

By Umer Tariq, founderPublished 30 October 20265 min read

The first question an American business owner asks us is rarely about price. It is about the clock. If the studio is in Karachi, who answers when something breaks at two in the afternoon in Ohio? It is the right question, and the honest answer has a cost side and a benefit side.

The short version

Drifted is a studio in Karachi, Pakistan, working with small businesses in the United States. Karachi runs ahead of every US timezone, so the team works while clients sleep and the founder keeps New York hours. Clients send a brief in the evening and wake up to the work. Live chat in the US afternoon is the trade-off.

How far apart are Karachi and the United States?

Pakistan keeps one time all year and does not move its clocks. The United States does. So the gap to New York is nine hours in summer and ten in winter, and it grows by another three hours to the West Coast. In practice, the Karachi working day runs through the American night and finishes as the East Coast is starting its morning.

What does the time difference cost a client?

Instant back-and-forth during the American afternoon. If your way of working is to message an editor, see a change ten minutes later and message again, a studio on the other side of the world will feel slow for exactly that kind of work. A small fix asked for at three in the afternoon in Chicago will usually be done when you wake up, not before you leave the office.

It also costs a little discipline. A brief that would be fine in a hallway conversation has to be written down, because the person doing the work cannot lean over and ask what you meant.

What does it give a client?

A working day that happens while you are not working. Send the brief, the photos or the feedback before dinner, and the edit, the page or the revised ad is waiting in the morning. For a small business whose owner reviews marketing between other jobs, that rhythm suits how they already live: decide in the evening, review over coffee.

And it gives a founder who keeps your clock. Umer, who founded the studio and sets the plan on every account, works from Karachi on New York time, so the calls happen in your working morning and the person on the call is the person responsible for the work.

What are the working rules that make it hold?

  1. Every request is written down, in one place, with what done looks like. Voice notes are fine; they get written up.
  2. A handover note closes each Karachi day: what shipped, what is waiting on the client, and what is next.
  3. Every client has one named person who answers for the account, so nothing waits on a team deciding whose turn it is.
  4. Calls are booked in the client's timezone, never ours, and every date we give is in the client's timezone too.
  5. Anything urgent goes to the founder, who is awake on your clock.

The team behind those rules is small and named on /about: Zayan runs operations, Aahan heads creative, Sana sets content strategy, Maham art directs, Khizer and Ali edit and animate, and Hamza leads business development. The studio is at Murtaza Commercial, DHA, Karachi, and the address is on /contact.

What does a normal week look like?

A client in New York sends feedback on a set of ad edits on Monday evening. While they sleep, Khizer recuts the edits in Karachi, Aahan reviews them against the brief, and Zayan checks they match what was agreed before anything goes out. On Tuesday morning, New York time, the client finds the revised edits, a short note of what changed and why, and one question that needs their answer. If the answer is quick, it goes back the same morning and the final versions arrive on Wednesday morning.

Nothing about that week needed a call. When one is needed, it is booked for the client's morning with Umer, who has the whole account in front of him. The pattern holds because the handover note is written every day, whether or not anything went wrong.

The thing a client notices most after a month is not the time difference. It is that work arrives in the morning, already reviewed, with the decision that needs them written at the top.

When is a studio on the other side of the world the wrong choice?

When your work needs someone physically there: a shoot at your shop, an event on the day, a person at the trade-show booth. Or when your marketing runs on live reaction during your afternoon, such as a support inbox or a fast-moving launch day. For those, hire locally and use a distant studio, if at all, for the slower, heavier work behind it. Our page for American businesses, at /us-brands, says what we take on and what we do not.

Questions we get asked

What time is it in Karachi when it is morning in New York?

Late afternoon or early evening, depending on the season, since Pakistan does not change its clocks and the United States does.

Will I ever have to take calls at night?

No. Calls are booked in your working hours. The founder keeps New York time for exactly that reason.

How quickly do you reply?

Within your working day for anything that needs a person, and urgent problems go straight to the founder. Production work is usually ready the next morning, your time.

Is Drifted a US company?

No. Drifted is a sole proprietorship registered in Pakistan, working with clients in the United States and elsewhere.
