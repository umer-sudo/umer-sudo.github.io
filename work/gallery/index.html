The Gallery

Work / Pakistan · personalised newborn brand

Baby blankets with a name stitched on, sold one conversation at a time.

Meta ads · content · targeting · shop

The problem

Nobody buys a blanket with their baby's name on it without asking a few questions first.

Which spelling. Which colour. Will it arrive before the date. Personalised newborn gifts sell through conversation, so the real checkout was always the DM, never the product page.

An ad built to push clicks at a cart would have been measuring the wrong thing. We had to build the campaign around the chat.

What we built

Ads that start a chat, and everything the chat lands on.

  • Creative made to open a conversation

    Every piece of the campaign set was built to make a parent ask a question, not to close a cart on the spot.

  • Campaigns aimed at the inbox

    Meta campaigns optimised toward conversations, with each sale counted where it actually happens: in the DMs.

  • Targeting, content and the shop

    The audience work, the posts and reels between campaigns, and a revamp of the shop at thegallery.pk.

  • One crew, every week

    Sana, Aahan and Zayan ran the account: the weak ads cut every week, the strong ones rebuilt, the result reported against what was agreed.

The hard parts

The sale happens in a chat, so the hard part was counting it.

  • A checkout with no cart

    Orders close in a conversation. We tracked the campaign against the inbox, so a good ad was judged by the chats it started and the orders they became, not by page visits.

  • No two orders are the same

    Personalisation is the product, so the creative leads with the embroidery itself. The craft is what a parent is buying.

  • Holding the return, every campaign

    One good month is easy. The record covers every campaign to July 2026, because the weak ads came out every week.

Where it is now

The record is written down, dated and public.

The playbook stands: DM-first ads, content made to start a conversation, and a shop that answers the questions before a parent has to ask.
