Gettysburg Museum of History

Work / Gettysburg, Pennsylvania · since August 2026

A museum full of real objects, and every one of them has a story.

Short film · campaign · search

The problem

People scroll straight past museums.

The museum keeps its collection on open display: uniforms, equipment and the stories of the men who carried them. None of that reaches someone who has not walked through the door.

The job was to put the collection in front of new people, and to fill the room for the museum's October anniversary weekend.

What we built

Films from the cases, then a thirty-day campaign, then the pages people find.

  • Short films from the collection

    We film the piece, write one line and cut it vertical: who wore it, where, and why it sits behind this glass.

  • The October campaign

    A thirty-day run for the Band of Brothers anniversary weekend: countdown stories, an object series, an invitation film and the paid plan, all in one look.

  • Search

    The event page, the museum's listing and the Foundation's new site, written so people searching for the weekend land on the museum's own page.

The wall

Hung like the collection it sells.

Every piece we made, on the wall, each with its own label.

The hard parts

A real jacket is only interesting if you know whose it was.

  • One true line per object

    Each film lives or dies on a single sentence. It has to be true to the museum's record, short enough to read in a second, and strong enough to stop a thumb.

  • Thirty days, one look

    Countdown, object series, invitation and paid plan were made as one campaign, so a stranger who saw three of them in a week knew they were the same museum.

  • Old photographs, new feed

    Archive pictures of Easy Company had to sit next to modern type without looking like a history lesson. A serif, bone paper and one red rule did it.

Where it is now

The work is ongoing.

The films keep coming from the collection, one object at a time. Each new piece from the cases becomes the next film.
